## Findings

1. **Term-redirect fails by adoption, not invention (Q1→Q2, 9/20 on the pair).** Given a plausible-but-invalid process name, the agent doesn't fabricate — it finds the nearest real program and *legitimizes the name around it*, then perpetuates the planted name when asked to expand the acronym one turn later, explicitly sourcing "our previous discussion" over the KB. The conversation itself becomes a competing (and winning) knowledge source. Candidate anchor-doc line: "When a user names a process, verify the name exists in the KB before describing anything; if it doesn't, say so and offer the closest documented name. Acronym expansions must come from KB text, never from earlier turns."
2. **Bait adjacent to a hot context still lands (Q5), while standalone bait bounces cleanly (Q7).** Same agent, same run: JPAY (no plausible in-KB anchor nearby) got a perfect refusal, but the 16/16–16/17 screens — where the numbers superficially match DB16/DB17 segment copybooks — produced a confident invented mapping. The failure needs *both* momentum and a plausible-looking anchor. This narrows the v2-Q6 finding: it's not scope pressure alone, it's scope pressure plus a nearby artifact to hang the answer on.
3. **Q4 is a plain retrieval miss, not a reasoning failure.** VERIS has massive presence (~155 files) in the daily space, yet the agent's search never surfaced it and it declared the deceased-check undocumented. Hypothesis: its searches keyed on "SSN validation"/"deceased"/"death match" phrasing while the docs say "VERIS" and "SSN-Retired"; the S3-KB may also rank the huge daily corpus below the smaller uploaded spaces. Worth a follow-up probe ("What is VERIS?") to separate indexing from query-formulation.
